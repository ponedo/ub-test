# Shared return-port service lower bound (independently reviewed, conditional PASS)

Prepared 2026-10-21 06:17 UTC from the accepted historical v3 source/data, not new B outcomes. The additional feedback-port hypothesis was recorded at 06:05:16 UTC. This is a model-specific necessary-service argument, not a parameter-optimization result, an A performance result, or a unique mediator decomposition.

## Claim, scope and arithmetic

For accepted Multicast group8, all four receiver members must receive 18 MiB, implemented as N=18432 WRITE TAs per member. With Jetty capacity W=512, at least N-W=17920 TA completions per member must be acknowledged before that member's final TA admission. Each such completion has a distinct native TAACK response; under the audited ordering path its own distinct request transport ACK (TPACK) must already have traversed the same switch egress3.

Therefore at least 4 × 17920 × 2 = 143360 distinct return packets have each consumed one original nonoverlapping 5000-ps allocator interval on switch64/port3 before all four final admissions. Necessary allocator service is at least 716800000 ps = 716.8 µs, within the weight-phase interval. Actual accepted weight time is 717182855 ps = 717.182855 µs. The 382855-ps difference is slack between a lower bound and an observed result, NOT an unexplained additive causal residual and NOT an A speedup prediction.

The argument is not inferred by dividing measured time by 5 ns. It follows from admission conservation, per-TA protocol generation, preserved packet ordering and single-port service exclusivity. Observed counts/timelines independently corroborate it. An independent source challenge review completed at 2026-10-21 06:27 UTC with conditional PASS: the counting, packet ordering, single-port nonoverlap and temporal-containment obligations below hold for the audited accepted configuration.

## Proof obligations and audited evidence

1. Fixed work and admission conservation.
   - accepted multicast-groups.csv: group8 has exactly source3→{5,13,52,59}, four full 18874368-byte transfers, no relay dependencies; multicast-stats.csv has 18432 source envelopes, fanout4 and 18432 first-send grants.
   - config runtime.segment_bytes=1024, native MTU4096. A WRITE TA and its zero-logical-byte/one-carrier-byte TAACK each use one native data packet. Accepted physical packet counts independently match 73728 logical request/response TAs.
   - UbJetty default UbJettyInflightMax=512 (ub-function.cc:1025–1029), actual IsLimited compares next−una with this capacity; transaction continuation exits when full (ub-transaction.cc:823–841). Admission commits increment next exactly once (ub-function.cc:1201–1237; transaction.cc:1225–1340). Before committing TA18432, next=18431 and next−una<512, hence una≥17920. The post-admission equivalent is next=18432 and next−una≤512.
   - ProcessInboundTaResponse validates the correlated response/source/destination/request/opcode before ProcessWqeSegmentComplete (transaction.cc:2114 onward). Jetty SSN advancement marks the single response's SSN, advances only contiguous marked entries and retries admission (ub-function.cc:1446–1510). There is no cumulative TAACK releasing unreceived SSNs.
   - No predecessor or reverse application weight WQE exists on these pairs. Dispatch/combine is gated until weights finish; state starts at zero. No cancellation/failure/early cleanup is enabled. Successful final completion cannot bypass unsent TA admissions. Fresh direct SSN/cleanup evidence is an additional check, not assumed available from the old recorder.

2. Every relevant request generates a separate native transport ACK before its TAACK is constructible.
   - RecvDataPacket (ub-transport.cc:2531–2660) updates contiguous receive state, builds a new transport response packet, EnqueueTransportResponse(...,"ack",...) and only then calls CompleteInboundTaUnits.
   - With retransmission disabled, BuildCurrentAckDecision (1793–1803) requests the ordinary cumulative ACK. Cumulative PSN content does NOT imply physical ACK coalescing: BuildTransportResponsePacket creates a new Packet; EnqueueTransportResponse appends with m_ackQ.push (2220–2305). Search of every m_ackQ mutation finds only this push, FIFO pop and teardown reset, no replacement/merge.
   - CompleteInboundTaUnits dispatches the request to the synchronous target executor (2512–2525). Transaction response is created only after execution completes (transaction.cc:2009–2100); WRITE resolves to TAACK (ub-transaction-rules.cc:28–32,69). Response maintains request identity and has NeedsTransactionResponse=false, so receiving it causes a transport ACK but no infinite transaction-ACK chain.

3. Corresponding TAACK cannot overtake its request TPACK before egress3.
   - Both use the existing reverse pair TP, destination source3. The request TPACK is already queued before the TAACK exists. GetNextPacket pops m_ackQ before TryGetNextNewDataPacket (ub-transport.cc:881–939); PopQueuedPacket uses std::queue front/pop (601–609). Thus the corresponding TPACK is popped before that TAACK's data packet. Older traffic may interleave but cannot reverse this pair.
   - Pair construction has priority7. TAACK packet construction writes m_priority into both VL fields (ub-transport.cc:1210–1238); TPACK copies the request PacketVL/CreditTargetVL (2220–2269), also7. Neither is the link-credit control frame at VL0.
   - Target has one physical outgoing port. Original egress FIFO and link serialization preserve order: ub-port.cc DoEnqueue:61–83 and front/pop:136–157, DequeuePacket:552–603 and TriggerTransmit:1068–1103 enforce FIFO and BUSY exclusion; TransmitPacket:829–899 and ub-link.cc:174–199 preserve order with serialization plus fixed propagation. Fixed star topology routes both from target i through physical switch ingress i and outport3 to NPU3 (accepted layer.cc:290–291).
   - Ordinary native ACK packets are PACKET_IPV4, not multicast V2 envelopes, and follow the normal URMA forwarding branch (ub-switch.cc:726–774). Switch ForwardDataPacket takes PacketVL as priority (ub-switch.cc:989–1024), then keys VOQ by (output,priority,input) (394–432). The two frames therefore enter the same (3,7,i) FIFO. Fixed zero input-processing delay cannot reorder them (81–86,456–501). UbPacketQueue push/front/pop preserves their order (ub-ingress-queue.cc:148–209). RR interleaving among four input queues cannot reverse ordering inside one queue.
   - With no losses/retransmission and fixed routing, the ordinary in-order path creates one TPACK per request. Lifecycle explicitly records native_retry_packets=0 and injected_losses=0; formal command has no retransmission/fault flags. Successful whole payload/TA verification excludes a missing unacknowledged request as a claimed completion. No source-frontier-to-packet-count shortcut is used without these invariants.

4. Distinct frames require distinct nonoverlapping original service intervals.
   - ScheduleAllocation sets a per-output allocationPending flag and asserts no other callback is pending, then schedules m_allocationTime=5ns (ub-switch-allocator.cc:110–114,188–218). TriggerAllocator coalesces while running (355–385).
   - The only production call to AllocateNextPacket is RunAllocation (251–266); direct zero-time calls found elsewhere are test fixtures, not the formal application. RR callback pops one packet and enqueues one egress entry (708–835), never a batch of ACKs.
   - A required native TPACK/TAACK cannot be transmitted without this enqueue. Different callback service intervals on port3 do not overlap. TX may overlap allocation; this does not invalidate a lower bound based only on the serialized allocator. Do not add TX service again.
   - Required feedback is generated only after weight submission and data delivery. A relevant return packet must traverse source→switch→target and then target→switch, with fixed 20 ns propagation on each of three links. Thus its switch-return callback cannot complete before at least 60 ns after weight start=201000ps (even ignoring serialization and other delays). Subtracting its fixed 5 ns callback duration leaves the entire interval strictly inside the phase. This end-minus-duration argument does not assume the packet was present when a callback began. All required frames finish allocator service before their TAACK is accepted and hence before final admission. The bound therefore fits inside the measured weight span.

## What this does not establish

The 3136 Gbps configuration is one logical aggregate port with one unchanged 5 ns allocator pipeline (at most 200M successful single-frame callbacks/s). A 62-byte ACK serializes in 158 ps in this model. Thus bitrate and packet-service capacity have not been scaled together. This theorem is not a CloudMatrix384 hardware ACK-throughput or multicast-capacity bound, and does not establish that 14 physical UB ports share one such pipeline. No AllocationTime or port-count change is authorized or proposed in this round.

The independent review is conditional on the same work, capacity, original 5 ns single-port service, path/order, and the absence of feedback aggregation, early cleanup or cancellation. No new performance intervention is implied. A remains stopped, with no measured benefit. The 382.855 ns observed-minus-bound slack is at most an upper bound on possible weight-time decrease for interventions that retain every premise of this bound; it is not a measured or predicted benefit of A, B, or any unrun change. A valid B outcome still identifies only the total changed-window effect. The bound does not prove that all source3 null callbacks or uncovered idle are one cause, nor a unique percentage of the MC−Chunk reversal. It also does not say every 1024/2048 outcome equals the substituted expression: different TP/TA frontiers, queues and bottlenecks can bind. A TP1000 claim requires actual branch evidence; cumulative ACK frontiers alone are not packet counts outside the audited one-to-one no-loss path.
